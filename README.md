# Multi-Touch-Attribution-MTA-

Multi-Touch Attribution (MTA) analyzes customer journeys across multiple marketing channels to determine how much each touchpoint contributes to a final conversion. Instead of giving all credit to the first or last interaction, MTA uses probabilistic or algorithmic methods (like Markov chains) to evaluate the influence of Email, Paid Search, Social, Direct, etc. By modeling how users move from one channel to another and measuring the impact of removing a channel, MTA provides a more accurate view of channel effectiveness and helps optimize marketing investment.


#When customers start an online account application, they might enter from different paths:
Paid search ad → landing page
Email → application start
Mobile app deep link
Homepage banner
Branch referral → online
Social ad → product page
Each touchpoint influences whether the customer actually completes the application.
MTA helps you quantify the impact of each entry point on successful account openings and identify which paths produce higher conversion and lower drop-offs.

##**Using MTA, you can answer:**
Which entry channel drives the highest quality traffic?
Which combinations of touchpoints lead to higher completion?
Do customers who start from paid search often drop off?
Does email + web retargeting produce more completed applications than email alone?
Should you increase investment in branch referrals because they have higher incremental conversions?


I applied Multi-Touch Attribution (MTA) to analyze customer journeys into the Online Account Opening flow, measuring the contribution of each entry channel to the final conversion. This helped optimize channel investment, improve digital funnel performance, and prioritize fixes in high-drop-off paths.



##**Key Online Account Opening (OAO) KPIs**
1. Application Start Rate
What it measures:
Percentage of users who reach the “start application” step after landing on your OAO entry point.

**How MTA supports it:**
Identifies which channels most effectively drive high-intent traffic to the start page
Uncovers which touchpoint sequences (e.g., Email → Direct → App Start) produce the cleanest funnel
Business value:
You can prioritize investment in channels that consistently deliver high-start-rate journeys.

2. Application Completion Rate
What it measures:
How many users who start an application actually finish it.

**How MTA supports it:**
Measures which channel combinations produce completed applications
Validates whether certain acquisition paths generate low-quality traffic
Helps you segment by channel, device, and entry point
Business value:
Improve budget allocation by focusing on channels that convert to completed accounts, not just clicks.

3. Fall-Out / Drop-Off Rate
What it measures:
Where users exit in the application workflow (KYC, document upload, funding step, etc.).

**How MTA supports it:**
Shows which entry channels produce users who drop off early vs. late
Helps identify friction points tied to acquisition sources
Supports prioritizing UX or technical fixes in specific steps
Business value:
You can reduce wasted marketing dollars by fixing the customer experience for high-volume, high-drop-off segments.

4. Cross-Sell Rate
What it measures:
How many completed applications result in additional products (e.g., savings + credit card).

**How MTA supports it:**
Evaluates which channel sequences lead to more valuable customers
Allows modeling long-term CLV differences by entry point
Helps you spot multi-product journeys
Business value:
Optimize which channels you use to target high-value segments.

5. Conversion Cost / Cost per Account
What it measures:
Marketing cost divided by successful account openings.

**How MTA supports it:**
Assigns accurate credit to each channel’s incremental contribution
Avoids double-counting conversions
Identifies channels that appear good on last-click but don’t drive true incremental conversion
Business value:
Shift budget to channels with higher true ROI.
